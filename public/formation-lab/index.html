Formation Lab

A play designer for youth flag football coaches. Draw a play in sixty seconds, watch it run, and send it to the team.

01 The brief

Youth flag football coaches call plays from a phone on the sideline, with a minute between snaps and often one bar of signal.

The tools they had were built for pro playbooks: depth charts, game-day call sheets, and a dozen menus between a coach and the play they wanted to show. The first TestFlight build of Formation Lab leaned the same way.

One piece of tester feedback changed the product. It became the spec, and the app was cut back to what a coach needs between snaps.

“I just want to call plays, not manage a whole pro football gameday callsheet.”

A coach, on TestFlight build 1

02 Key decisions

  1. A field and a fingertip.

    Pick a formation and drag routes onto the field. Formations match the league: 5v5, 7v7 or 9v9, with positions labeled the way the team calls them.

  2. Plays that move.

    Every play animates from snap to catch. Coaches export a play, or a whole playbook, as a short video that drops straight into the team group chat.

  3. Paper still matters.

    Call sheets print one to twelve plays a page, and wristband cards print at actual size, eight plays to a card.

  4. Built for the sideline.

    Everything works offline. A playbook per season keeps last fall’s plays out of this spring’s, and the app stores no player information at all.

05 How it’s built

Formation Lab is a native SwiftUI app for iPhone and iPad. Plays are drawn by a rendering engine that lives in its own pure-Swift package, with no UIKit, so the same code draws the canvas, the animation, the exported video and the printed page.

The web editor at formationlab.app/draw runs a TypeScript port of that engine, held to the Swift engine’s golden snapshots. A play draws the same on a phone, in a browser and on paper.
