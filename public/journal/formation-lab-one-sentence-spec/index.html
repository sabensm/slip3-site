November 13, 2026 · Formation Lab

The one-sentence spec.

Formation Lab doesn’t have a product requirements document, and that’s deliberate. One line of feedback replaced it.

The first TestFlight build of Formation Lab leaned the way football software usually does, toward how professional teams organize a game day.

Then a coach sent this:

“I just want to call plays, not manage a whole pro football gameday callsheet.”

That was the whole spec. The people using the app coach youth flag football. They stand on a sideline with a phone, a minute between snaps, and a group of kids who need to see where to run. Anything that didn’t serve that moment came out.

What survived

  • A field and a fingertip. Pick a formation, drag the routes. A play takes about a minute.
  • Plays that move. Every play animates from snap to catch, because kids learn a play by watching it.
  • The group chat. Plays export as short videos, ready for iMessage or WhatsApp, so parents and players see exactly what the coach sees.
  • Paper, at the right size. Call sheets print one to twelve plays a page, and wristband cards print at actual size.
  • The sideline. Everything works offline, with a playbook per season and tags to find a play mid-huddle.

Keeping the rule

Without a document, the decisions live in the project’s notes, each with the reason behind it. When a new idea comes up, the test is still that sentence: does this help a coach call plays?

It has held through eight releases since September, up to version 2.0. The case study covers the rest, including the engine that draws a play the same way on a phone, in a browser and on paper.
