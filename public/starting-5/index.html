01 The brief

Daily games work when they are short, shared, and the same for everyone. Basketball fans didn’t have one built for them.

The Starting 5 gives every fan the same five players each day, with the day turning over at midnight UTC so London and Los Angeles share one game and one leaderboard. Twelve questions, all answered from those five, against a ten-second shot clock. A game takes about a minute.

The goal was a game people come back to every morning, compare in the group chat, and keep a career in over a whole season.

02 Key decisions

  1. A shot clock, not a quiz.

    Ten seconds a question: three points with seven or more seconds left, two with four to six, one after that. The fourth quarter pays double, and a clean sweep of a quarter earns a bonus.

  2. One game a day.

    No infinite feed. Today’s five, a live daily leaderboard, and a box score built to be shared in the group chat.

  3. A career, not a streak counter.

    Career stats, seasons, shot charts, quarter breakdowns and achievements turn a daily minute into a long game.

  4. No ads. Just ball.

    Free to play, with no ads and no pay-to-retry. Anyone can play a warm-up game without an account; a free account puts you on today’s leaderboard and keeps your career across devices.

    06 How it’s built

    Every game is generated overnight by an automated pipeline. It picks the day’s five, checks each one against the player database before doing anything else, researches each career with search grounded on basketball-reference.com, and writes twelve questions with exactly one right answer among the five. A rules check and an independent fact-check review every game; if either fails, the questions are regenerated, and after three misses the game is held for human review instead of shipping broken.

    The web game and the iPhone app share one real-time backend, so a career, a streak and the leaderboard are the same everywhere.
